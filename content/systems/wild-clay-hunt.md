## The curiosity

Almost every town has a story in its clay: a name, an industry that rose and died on it, a thing people still use. Nobody tells it, because it sits in survey bulletins and county histories. I wanted to tell one a week, and end each one with a question for the reader: what is under yours?

## What it does

Each post is one place and one turn in its story, checked against its sources. It stands on its own: no series to follow, no dig of mine needed, nothing for sale. It ends with one invite: reply with your town and I will look up what is under it.

## The system

Every Sunday at 9, my computer picks a place it has not written about, researches it, checks the facts, and writes the post and a short note. It saves the post in Substack as a draft and posts the note on its own. I read the post, edit it, and publish it. The post never goes out without me.

## Cause and effect

The input was a place name: Kaolin, a spot on Route 41 in Chester County. The output was a town named after porcelain clay, pits that closed when Georgia's clay won on price, and a question for the reader. That is the letter above, and the same ground is [on the map](wild-clay-archive).

## What changed

This used to be a hunt log: one long search through old reports, in the order I found things. It read like my notes. Now each post is a story about somewhere a reader can picture, and a door into the map. The old hunt logs are paused.
